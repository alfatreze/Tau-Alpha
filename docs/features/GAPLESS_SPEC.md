# Gapless playback: spec and sizing (B-723, 2026-10-14)

**Status: DESIGN + HOST MODEL + one measurement. Nothing built.** Parent plans: `CYMO_HEADPHONE_PLAN.md` section 8 (gapless re-scoped), `CYMO_AUDIO_ENGINE.md` F5/C4/C6, `AUDIO_FIRST_TRACK_LOAD_SPEC.md` (the load sequence). Model: `tools/lab/gapless_model.py`. Evidence labels: [READ] source, [HW] measured on a Pocket, [HOST] measured on the host files, [EST] estimate.

## 1. What gapless needs, in one line
At a natural track end the next track's first sample must reach the DAC before the last buffered sample of the old track has played: **buffered audio at the moment the old decoder reaches end of file > the load of the next track**, and the boundary must neither flush the buffer nor fade, and must cut the encoder's delay and padding.

## 2. Facts
- **Buffer today [READ]:** the hardware FIFO is 2,048 stereo pairs (46 ms at 44.1 kHz). `load_track()` begins with `pcm_flush()` (empties it and arms the fade-in) and runs synchronously: nothing is pushed until the next track's first frames are prefilled.
- **Load time [HW]:** a typical album track is **263 to 292 ms** (LOAD MS total, B-562: head 117 + size 18 + cover 12 to 115 + prefill about 120 to 170), a FLAC with a large metadata block **1,170 ms** (1.1 s head read, B-544). The audio-first spec's 3.7 s load with cover decode is history (TIM1 covers take about 90 ms, B-330). A manual change measured **1.48 s** press to sound (B-717, includes the press, the flush and the 44 ms fade-in); that is not a gapless case, a manual skip keeps its flush and fade.
- **Trim rule [HOST, proven here]:** `playable = frames x 1152 - delay - padding`, from the Xing/Info frame count and the LAME delay/padding (`fw/lame_tag.h`: skip `delay + 529` at the start, drop `padding - 529` at the end). On all **six** Test Album MP3 files (MacCunn and Clementi, 128 and 320 kbps, 44.1 and 48 kHz; delay 576, padding 832 to 1,668) the result equals the **FLAC sample count of the same recording exactly** (7,708,092, 7,345,884, 8,389,760, 7,995,520: difference 0). FLAC needs no trim (STREAMINFO has the exact count). Files without a LAME tag (other encoders) are played as today, gap and all.
- **Output chain [READ]:** gain stage fades only on flush or `FADE_NOW`; the Cymo resampler and the feed queue run on (no clear) when the rate is unchanged; the Cymo guard (44.1 kHz at 1.00x) follows `pcm_rate_apply()` and is unchanged across a same-rate boundary.

## 2b. I/O check of the 2 s figure (B-724, `analogue-pocket-dev` skill, KB-139/KB-040/KB-043, firmware read)
- **SD path [HW, KB-139]:** a core reads a data slot through the host target-read command at about **850 KB/s** (4 KB chunks, one command in flight: 4.8 ms average, 13 ms worst, latency-bound, not bandwidth-bound). Decode-ahead at 1.25x reads FLAC at about 160 KB/s and 320 kbps MP3 at about 50 KB/s: **under 20% of the path**, so decoding ahead is not an I/O problem. A 4 KB read is 0.4% of a 2 s ring, so the ring absorbs any SD hiccup the normal path can show (and the card's own garbage-collection stalls, hundreds of ms, which today underrun a FLAC after only 24 KB / 700 kbps = 0.27 s of compressed data).
- **What the load time really is [READ]:** a track change is a core-issued `0192` slot open (KB-043) followed by a **settle check** that reads the first 16 bytes twice about 30 ms apart and waits (up to about 1 s) until the new file's bytes are stable and different from the old (`read_track_head`). The 117 ms typical `ld_head` is that handshake. The one 1,170 ms reading (B-544) is most likely that loop running to its timeout, **not** a big head: FLAC picture blocks are skipped by a seek (`flac_skip_bytes`: no read, B-prior fix) and the Test Album heads are 160 to 285 KiB in any case (0.2 to 0.35 s at 850 KB/s if they were read). So the worst measured load is about 1.2 to 1.3 s; **one sample, cause unconfirmed**, which is exactly what G0 must measure.
- **PSRAM [HW, KB-040]:** 32 / 26 cycles per uncached read / write; a 2 s ring is 345 KiB of 16 MB per chip and about 88 k accesses per second (write plus read per pair), small against the instruction fetch the same chip already serves. No bandwidth limit.
- **Lead build time (new, the real cost of "2 s"):** the ring only protects a boundary if it is FULL when the old file ends. It fills at (decode speed - 1.0) seconds of audio per second: at the measured 44% busy a MP3 decodes at about 2.3x (lead builds 1.3 s/s, 2 s in 1.5 s), a hard stereo FLAC (idle 45% after the Rice path) about 1.8x (0.8 s/s, 2.5 s), but **paced at 1.25x to protect the UI the lead builds 0.25 s/s: 8 s to reach 2 s**. Consequences: after a manual change or a seek the first seconds have a short lead, a boundary reached in that time gaps by (load - lead), and a track shorter than the fill time (a 3 s jingle) never reaches a full lead. Pick the pacing from the Info CPU data (G0): a fast fill for the first seconds, then relaxed.
- **What 2 s does NOT change:** volume, ReplayGain at the stage, Halcyon, the meters (they tap the FIFO output), pause/resume (the ring keeps its content) and the response of Next/Previous/seek (flush as today; the first pairs of the new track are pushed as before). It adds a heard-position offset the UI must account for (section 5.1) and a CPU burst after a seek.

**Verdict on the figure.** 2 s is sized by the slowest load measured once (1.3 s) plus margin, not by an average: for typical loads (0.26 to 0.29 s) 0.5 s is enough. It is **provisional until G0** gives the distribution of the slot-open settle time over many tracks. A 1 s ring (172 KiB) would hide every typical load and most outliers and builds its lead in half the time; 2 s buys the 1+ s outlier at the price of a longer build. Do not fix the size before G0.

## 2c. Cover art must not block the loop (owner point, 2026-10-14; a gap in sections 2b and 5.5 above)
**Correction.** Section 5.5 said a seconds-long cover decode "still fits a 3 s ring". It does not: a full decode of an embedded JPEG measured **2.6 s (707 px, BRAM), 5.3 s (455 px) and 15.8 s (1400 px)** with the art accumulator in PSRAM (A-120, B-027), and `load_track()` runs it synchronously, so **the whole loop, audio push included, stops for that long**. TIM1 sidecar covers (`tau-art/*.timg`, pal256 128 px, 16.9 KB) take about 90 ms and are harmless; the problem is every album WITHOUT a sidecar (the embedded-JPEG fallback). A ring cannot hide 15 s and should not be sized for it. The cover has to become **a background job at the lowest priority**.
**Feasible, and the code is already shaped for it [READ]:** `art_decode()` (`fw/art.inc`) is a loop of `pjpeg_decode_mcu()` calls, one MCU (8x8 to 16x16 pixels) per call, pulling bytes through `art_need_bytes()` and accumulating a downscaled box average into `art_acc` (already in PSRAM, B-567) and flushing a row at a time to the stash. Nothing in it needs to run to completion in one call.
- **Design:** turn it into a resumable job `art_job_begin(track) / art_job_step(budget_cycles) / art_job_abort()`: the loop indices (MCU x/y, rows flushed, byte position) move into a small state struct in PSRAM; a step decodes MCUs until the budget (for example 1 to 2 ms) is spent and returns. The main loop calls one step per pass ONLY when the audio side is comfortable (the FIFO above a high-water mark, the PCM ring not below its low-water mark, no UI redraw pending): lowest priority by construction. A cover then takes 5 to 10 times longer (15 s becomes about a minute for a 1400 px file) but costs the music nothing; the old cover stays on screen and the new one fades in when the job finishes (the audio-first spec's plan, `AUDIO_FIRST_TRACK_LOAD_SPEC.md`). A track change, seek-free skip or a new track's cover aborts and restarts the job.
- **Bytes:** the JPEG sits inside the file being played, so its bytes come from the same single streaming slot at a different offset; each `art_need_bytes` refill is one 4 KB read command (4.8 ms, KB-139) issued between audio refills (one command in flight at a time: the job must not start a read while an audio refill is pending, and must be the loser when both want the slot).
- **Fast first paint:** picojpeg's `reduce` mode (DC only, 1/8 scale) is far cheaper; a coarse preview first and the full-quality pass later is possible but optional.
- **Better than any of it:** give every album a sidecar (`sync_media.py --art-variants`, Tau Omega), so the fallback is rare; the background job then only protects libraries that were not prepared.
- **Order:** the async cover is a prerequisite of G2 (the gapless boundary), not an extra: without it a boundary into an album with an embedded cover still stops the music for seconds. New step **G1b** in section 6 (after G1, before G2); the ring sizing in section 3 then only has to cover the non-cover load (head, settle, size probe, prefill).

## 3. The sizing result (the reason the plan is what it is)
`python3 tools/lab/gapless_model.py` (gap = max(0, load - buffer depth), 44.1 kHz):

| Buffer | M10K | gap for 263 ms | 292 ms | 1,170 ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| today's FIFO, 2,048 pairs | 7 | 217 ms | 246 | 1,124 |
| RTL FIFO 8,192 pairs (186 ms) | 26 | **77 ms** | 106 | 984 |
| RTL FIFO 16,384 pairs (372 ms) | 52 | 0 | 0 | 798 |
| PSRAM ring 0.5 s (86 KiB) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 670 |
| PSRAM ring 1.5 s (258 KiB) | 0 | 0 | 0 | **0** |
| PSRAM ring 2 to 3 s (345 to 517 KiB) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

**The deeper RTL FIFO that the earlier plan proposed (8,192 pairs) does not make a typical track gapless (77 ms gap left) and 16,384 pairs takes 52 of the 68 free M10K for a result that still gaps on the slow FLAC.** A ring of decoded PCM in PSRAM, 1.5 to 2 s deep, covers every measured load with margin, costs no block RAM and no RTL.

## 4. Recommended architecture: a decoded-PCM ring in PSRAM (firmware only)
- **Producer / consumer split.** The decoder writes PCM into a PSRAM ring (`0xA4...`, a new region next to the tempo staging ring at `0xA4600000`; 2 s at 44.1 kHz stereo is 345 KiB, 48 kHz 375 KiB); a drain step moves pairs from the ring into the hardware FIFO whenever it has room. This is the structure the tempo funnel already uses (`fw/tempo_core.h`: planar PSRAM staging ring, drain through the push path, host-tested), so the mechanism is proven and the same drain serves MP3 and FLAC.
- **Boundary = a marker in the ring.** At end of file the decoder opens the next track WITHOUT `pcm_flush()` and without the loader UI (`load_track_gapless()`), applies the trim, and keeps writing into the same ring behind a boundary marker carrying the next track's identity. The drain pops the marker when it reaches it and only then switches the UI, the ReplayGain target (the stage's 5 ms ramp) and the position clock: the screen follows what is heard, not what is decoded.
- **Rules at a boundary:** same sample rate only (the FIFO drain rate is global; a rate change keeps today's flush path); no `FADE_NOW`, no `pcm_flush`; the Cymo guard and the resampler state untouched; a manual Next/Previous/seek/stop flushes the ring (the hardware FIFO too) and fades in as today.
- **Cost [EST]:** one extra PSRAM write and read per pair, measured access cost 26 and 32 cycles (B-022): about 58 cycles of about 1,512 per pair at 44.1 kHz and 66.667 MHz, **about 4% of the CPU**; the hard stereo FLAC (idle 45% after the Rice fast path, B-562) can afford it. Decode-ahead must be paced (for example 1.25x real time) so the first 2 s of a track do not saturate the CPU and starve the UI and the meters.
- **Side benefit, independent of gapless:** a 1.5 to 2 s buffer absorbs SD stalls and UI bursts, the biggest lever on non-decode underruns (`CYMO_AUDIO_ENGINE.md` F5); it would also hide the slow-head FLAC loads at a manual change if the user accepted the position lag (not planned).
- **Not recommended:** a second decoder (RAM), a deeper RTL FIFO as the main fix (section 3), cross-rate gapless (needs a drain-rate change; a 44.1 to 48 kHz boundary stays a short gap).

## 5. Open design points (decide before building)
1. **Playback position and seek.** The decode position runs up to 2 s ahead of the heard position; the clock, the progress bar and seeking must use the heard position (decode position minus the ring fill). Seek flushes the ring.
2. **Pause.** The ring keeps its content; resume plays it (no re-decode). Stop and track-end-with-repeat-one behave as today.
3. **Which track is next.** Library queue order, shuffle and repeat decide it (`lib_q`); the next track is chosen when the old file ends. A user skip before the boundary flushes everything (normal manual path).
4. **ReplayGain across the boundary.** The next track's tags are read at open, the target changes at the marker (ramp 5 ms), so two tracks with different gains meet without a step; with positive gain the cap of B-720 is per track.
5. **Cover and tags.** Decoded at open (about 90 ms with TIM1, up to a few hundred ms otherwise) while the ring plays; the display switches at the marker. A track with no tau-art cover and a big embedded JPEG (seconds) is the worst case and still fits a 3 s ring; it is the reason the ring is sized by the slowest measured load, not the average.
6. **Tempo.** The tempo funnel (MP3, speed above 1.00x) has its own staging ring; gapless with tempo is a later step and should reuse one ring.
7. **FLAC hi-res and refused files.** A refused file (96 kHz) ends the gapless chain: the old track plays out, the refusal screen appears after it.

## 7. Risks
- The ring and the drain touch every audio push site (MP3, FLAC, tempo) and the track-load path: the largest firmware change in the audio loop since the dB volume, so G1 ships alone and is heard before G2.
- PSRAM contention with instruction fetch for cold code and the art decode (the ring adds about 350 KB/s: small, but the Check soak must run with it).
- Hot RAM: the drain and the marker handling must live in cold code or fit the 1,120 B Diagnostic gap (it is tight, B-720 trim note); budget the first G1 build against `check_heap_gap.py` and a `DIAG_DROP` if needed.
- A wrong trim is audible as a click at every boundary; the six-file exact match (section 2) is the guard, the host stream test the gate.

## 8. G1 integration sites in `fw/player.c` (B-730, read from the source; nothing wired yet)
The ring core (`fw/pcm_ring.h`, host-tested) replaces the FIFO as the thing the decoders write to. Every site below must change together, which is why G1 ships alone behind `TAU_PCM_RING` (default 0) and is heard before G2.
| Site | Today | With the ring |
|---|---|---|
| MP3 push (`pcm_push_pairs`, ~10501) | gain, burst write to FIFO, waits on FIFO room | `pcm_ring_put` raw pairs; waits on ring room; `spin` = poll_input + refill_pump + **drain** |
| FLAC and tempo push (`cymo_push`, 7866) | per-pair gain + FIFO write | per-pair put into the ring (a block put from `flac_emit` is cheaper), no gain |
| Drain | n/a | `pcm_ring_drain` called from the main loop top, from every producer wait, and from `poll_input` callers that wait; **not while paused** (a paused ring keeps its content; the FIFO runs dry in 46 ms as today) |
| `pcm_flush()` (6916) | `R_PCM_ST = 1`, arm fade | also `pcm_ring_flush`; the fade-in now applies at the drain, so the first pairs HEARD fade in |
| Meters (`meters_feed`, 10497 MP3, 8004 FLAC, tempo_meter) | fed at decode time | **2 s early with a ring.** Move the feed to the drain: gather raw pairs into the 1152-pair capture buffer and call `meters_feed` per 1152 pairs, so the software peak/scope/wave data line up with the hardware spectrum and wave taps (which already read the FIFO output). Remove the decode-time calls under the macro |
| Clock and progress (`frames`, `ui_sec`) | decode position | heard position = decode position - (ring fill + FIFO level) in pairs; `ui_sec` advances from drained pairs |
| End of file (`gap_mark_eof`, 10307, 10352) | `lib_advance_auto()` loads the next track at once, which flushes | **G1 alone:** wait for the ring and the FIFO to play out, then continue exactly as today (otherwise the last 2 s of every track would be cut). G2 replaces this wait with the boundary marker |
| Underrun checks (`pcm_underrun`, `pcm_level`) | FIFO flags | unchanged (they read the FIFO); `min_level` and the stress counters keep their meaning; add the ring fill to Info (RING row) |
| HW GAIN hand-over (`fw/gain_handover.h`) | mutes, flushes through `gh_flush` = `pcm_flush` | works unchanged because `pcm_flush` empties the ring too; re-check the 16 ms wait |
| Pause resume (`fade_restart`) | fade on the next pushed pair | fade at the next drained pair (same function, applied at the drain) |
| Seek, Next, Previous, stop | flush | same flush, now also dropping up to 2 s of decoded audio (as intended) |
**Memory:** the ring is a PSRAM region (2 s of pairs at 4 bytes = 345 KiB at 44.1 kHz, 375 KiB at 48 kHz); the region must be added to the PSRAM map (`CYMO_TEMPO_INTEGRATION.md` section 4: tempo staging is at +6 MiB, library image, queue and Check scratch elsewhere) and proven at boot like the tempo ring. Drain and put are small; keep the producer's put inlined in the hot loop only if the heap gap allows, otherwise cold with a hot trampoline.
**Test plan:** the stream-equality host test exists for the core; add a host model of the sites above (pause, flush, EOF wait, heard position, meter alignment) before the firmware edit; Pocket: the usual Check, a loopback of a tone through a track change and a pause, and Info RING and GAP rows.

## 9. Adversarial review of the ring approach (B-730; embedded-audio and Pocket view, skill `analogue-pocket-dev`)
Evidence labels as above; KB ids are the skill's. Findings ranked by how much they could invalidate the plan.
1. **BLOCKER in the spec as written: a firmware drain protects nothing during the stalls the ring exists for.** The hardware FIFO is still 46 ms deep and only the CPU moves pairs from the ring into it. Any loop that runs without calling the drain for more than about 40 ms underruns exactly as today, however full the ring is. The loops that matter are the ones G2 is sized for: the slot-open settle wait in `read_track_head` (the 1,074 ms / 33-read outlier, a 30 ms spin that calls nothing), `target_read_slot` spins, the TIM1 decode, settings SD writes, the Settings crossfade and every full-box redraw (KB-086: a redraw longer than a frame). **Required:** (a) an audited list of every blocking loop reachable during playback, each calling the drain at under 20 ms intervals; (b) a diagnostic "longest time between drains" (Info row) so a missed loop shows up as a number, not an underrun by ear. **Escalation if (a) is not enough:** an RTL ring reader (a third PSRAM client reading the ring into the FIFO on its own, CPU only puts): removes the CPU copy and the whole class, costs an arbiter port and a fit (the PSRAM bus already has the two-client arbiter and all-corner margin). The earlier rejection of RTL options (section 3) only compared FIFO depth, not an autonomous PSRAM reader.
2. **High: the lead is not always there.** The ring is full only in steady state (producer blocked). After a start, seek or manual change it fills at (decode speed - 1.0) (KB-110/B-591 numbers: about 1.3 s/s for MP3, 0.25 s/s at 1.25x pacing): a natural end soon after a seek still gaps. Needs the fast-fill rule and a test that reports ring fill at EOF (Info), and tracks shorter than the fill time never gap-free. Already in section 2b; keep it a gate, not a note.
3. **High: the rate boundary is a hardware switch the ring must not cross early.** `pcm_rate_apply()` flips the Cymo guard (live only for 44.1 kHz at 1.00x) and the global drain rate; with 2 s of old-rate audio in the ring the switch must happen when the drain reaches the boundary marker, not at decode time. G1 alone is safe (play-out wait); G2 must carry the rate in the marker and refuse gapless across rates (as written).
4. **High: single-slot identity watchers.** The slot is switched to the next file while up to 2 s of the old one still plays. The identity gate, ID3 re-probe, `reload_armed` logic and `sw_prev_head` compare the slot's bytes against the playing track to detect a Pocket-menu reload; an early self-inflicted switch must not read as a user reload (and the reverse). Needs an explicit "switching" state; host-model it before G2.
5. **Medium: tempo.** The tempo funnel has its own staging ring and changes the input/output time mapping, so heard position, ring fill and seek differ from plain playback; spec says gapless-with-tempo is later: keep the ring off while tempo is active and say so in the UI.
6. **Medium: hot RAM and CPU.** About 58 cycles a pair (3.8% at 44.1 kHz) is in line with KB-040 (32/26 cycles) but is single-word uncached access with instruction fetch from the same chip (B-054 saw 380 vs 373 worst cycles): measure ring put/drain cost and worst access with the real cold-code load before trusting [EST]. KB-132 applies: a streaming buffer can cost more than it saves. The Diagnostic Build heap gap is 1.4 KB hot: put/drain/hooks must fit or go cold with a hot trampoline.
7. **Medium: meters and clock.** Already in section 8 (feed at the drain, heard position). The software meters being fed at decode time is the most visible regression if missed.
8. **Low: proof and fallback.** KB-039/KB-044: the ring region must be proven at boot and the feature must switch OFF (direct FIFO path), not misbehave, when the proof fails or the bitstream has no PSRAM window; that keeps two push paths alive in a hot loop.
9. **Low: what the ring does not fix.** Manual change keeps the flush and 44 ms fade (B-717: 1.48 s press to sound); the 560 ms natural-end gap includes that flush, the settle, the head read and prefill, so part of it may be reducible without any ring (skip the flush, shorten the settle): measure the phases of one natural end (LD phases are already counted) before building.
**Verdict:** the ring is sound as the container for lead audio, but the plan was incomplete: it assumed the CPU always gets back to the drain in time. Do not wire G1 until item 1(a) and 1(b) exist (drain-gap diagnostic first, a G0b build), and decide the RTL reader as the fallback up front rather than after a failed Pocket test.

# Layered Wave performance plan (D-T14)

Status 2026-11-06: **analysis done and a fix identified, not implemented, not on a Pocket.** Written at the owner's request ("review the Talos and Helios improvements, check if pending ones are relevant, deep-analyse Layered Wave to re-architect it for performance; history is poor, spectrum is much better"). Evidence labels: **[MODEL]** = host simulation with an instruction-cache model (below), **[HW]** = measured on a Pocket and recorded elsewhere, **[DOC]** = stated in a repo document, **[INF]** = my inference.

## 1. The finding
**History is slow mainly because of instruction-cache conflict misses, not because of drawing or arithmetic [MODEL, consistent with [HW]].** Layered Wave's code runs from PSRAM through the CPU's instruction cache, which is **4 KiB, direct-mapped, 32-byte lines** (`VexRiscv_Full.v`: one way, 128 tags; `docs/PHASE_G_SPEC.md`). A hit costs about nothing; a miss costs a line fill of about **253 cycles** (8 beats; 31.6 cycles per word [HW, B-054]). Layered Wave's `lw_tick` is one 7.6 KB function (the history, spectrum and curve views are all inlined into it), larger than the whole cache, and its small helpers (`lw_cell`, `lw_cr8`, `lw_win`, `lw_bch`, `lw_emit`) sit exactly 8 KiB before the history loop, so **every cell the history loop draws evicts the helper it is about to call and the helper evicts the loop**: about one miss per 16 instructions, a classic ping-pong. Under the model that is **87 % of the meter's time** (3.0 M misses for 69.6 M instructions over the shared trace, 253 cycles each, against 1.65 cycles per instruction for the rest).
**Spectrum is fast by accident of layout:** its loop body lies elsewhere in the 7.6 KB function, at addresses that do not collide with the helpers. Nothing in the design makes it better than history; reorder the link and the roles swap (section 3).

## 2. Evidence
**Method.** (Strides: the harness runs the host's command-count rule on random bands, which settles at a stride of about 3; on real music the shipped firmware sits at larger strides, so per-tick Pocket cycles are lower than the modelled ones for the same preset. See P1b.) The pack is built exactly as shipped (`tools/player2/pack_meter.py`, org 4 KiB-aligned like the real slot, so cache indices equal the Pocket's), run on `tools/rv32sim.py` with a copy that (a) counts instructions per function and (b) models the instruction cache (128 lines, direct-mapped, only addresses at or above 0x400000 = the pack). Per-tick cost is the marginal cost: (13 frames - 3 frames) / 10 of one preset in the 368 x 122 window box (fullscreen 400 x 323 is within 10 % of it); no forced repaints, no pause. Modelled cycles = instructions x 1.65 + misses x 253. **Not modelled:** the draw engine's own time, SDRAM contention, the PSRAM data window used by the history ring (about 19 k cycles per redraw, section 7), interference from other cold code between ticks.
**Does the model match the hardware?** The only Pocket figures: slowest tick since boot **7.66 M cycles** (DEV 173) and **5.2 M** (quoted as the worst tick in `docs/handoffs/SESSION_HANDOFF_2026-10-28_TALOS_SDRAM_CONTROLLER.md` and D-T14; the source shot is not named there) [HW]. The modelled history presets are 4.0 to 10.8 M: the same range. The model also says why: no smooth history preset can run in under 3.9 M cycles today. **Open mismatch:** the T4 report (DEV 195) shows an average of 48 k cycles (window) and 117 k (fullscreen) for Layered Wave [HW]; that is below the cheapest smooth history preset (PULSE, blocks, 0.79 M) and cannot be a smooth redraw: the preset on screen then is unknown to me, and the average includes ticks that draw nothing (BLOCKS redraws only when the history advanced). The check on a Pocket (section 6, step P0) settles it.

The "3-line split" variant adds `noinline` to `lw_history`, `lw_spectrum` and `lw_curve` and nothing else: one attribute per view function, no behaviour change. History presets drop 4.0-10.8 M to 0.41-1.07 M cycles (about 10x). The "split + inlined helpers" variant (the small helpers forced inline) cuts instructions by about 25 % but **GLOW [EX] regresses from 1.2 to 5.7 M**: a new collision elsewhere. Two lessons: the fix is small, and **any layout change must be guarded by a test that counts conflict misses per preset**, because a layout is fragile (a link order that makes history fast, `lw_tick` first, makes TIDE 6x and GLOW 8x worse: measured, not shown above).
**Other packs, same model (shared trace) [MODEL]:** Winamp Bars 0.6 % of modelled time in misses, Scope 0.2 %, Master VU 2.2 %, Chladni 9.2 %, Waterfall 5.5 %. **Layered Wave is the outlier (87 %); this is not a framework-wide problem.** Chladni (8.5 k misses) is the next candidate to look at, cheaply, with the same tool. The firmware's UI cold code was not modelled.
**Why it matters beyond the picture [INF]:** at 66.7 MHz 7.66 M cycles is 115 ms per tick, over three frames at 30 Hz, with the CPU busy the whole time (buttons, UI redraws, audio refill all wait). The meter's soft cycle limit is 15 M (`p2.max_cycles`), so it is never struck; the owner's "maybe slightly slower or a little jumpier" (DEV 198) fits.

## 3. What is left after the layout fix
With the 3-line split a history tick is 0.2 to 0.5 M instructions, 0.4 to 1.1 M cycles (6 to 16 ms of a 33 ms frame at 30 Hz), i.e. **within budget but still the heaviest meter** (Bars is about 14 k cycles). Where the instructions go (AURORA, window, [MODEL]): the history loop itself 48 %, `lw_cr8` (Catmull-Rom, once per cell) 23 %, `lw_cell` 13 % (a call per cell that does nothing unless blending or antialiasing is on), `lw_win` 8.5 %, divisions and remainders 1.5 % of instructions (the source has several per cell), multiplies 5 %. Over the shared four-preset trace (which includes blending presets) `lw_bch` (the blend arithmetic) adds 4.5 % and `lw_endlayer` 3.4 %. [INF] about 200 instructions per drawn cell (the stride that fixes the cell count was not logged). Engine time was never the limit for this meter (no fences, 112 to 240 commands, 50-85 k pixels a frame [DOC, T4]); after the CPU fix it should be re-measured, because rectangle height is the cost there (a tall 1-pixel-wide rectangle is one short burst per row).

## 5. Plan
**P0 - confirm on a Pocket (a pack file only: no firmware, no RTL, no fit).** Build the shipped Layered Wave pack and the 3-line-split pack as two `tau-packs.bin` and install each in turn on a DEV build (`tools/package_dev_build.py`, same bitstream). For the same preset (say AURORA, then NEON, then TIDE) read `METER MAX LAYERED WAVE` and the per-meter cost rows on Info. Prediction [MODEL]: history 4-10 M down to 0.4-1.1 M cycles (about 10x); spectrum unchanged. If the Pocket shows less than 3x, the model is wrong and nothing below should be built on it.
**P1 - make the layout robust (firmware only, pack source).** Split `lw_tick` into `noinline` `lw_history`, `lw_spectrum`, `lw_curve`; keep the hot loops and their helpers contiguous, each view under 4 KiB with no two hot lines on one cache index. Add a **host test with a conflict budget** (`sim/test_p2_icache.py`, the model above, per pack and per preset; fails above N misses per tick, like the heap floor) so a later edit cannot silently bring the 10x back, and run it for every pack (Chladni is the next suspect). No change to the picture: the golden test against the JS twin stays command-for-command identical.
**P1 status (B-946, host-simulated):** done on `lw-perf`. The split alone (DEV 205) left the curve presets conflicting (3,013 misses per tick at preset 12); the hot helpers and each view now have their own sections and the pack linker script starts each view where the hot block ends modulo 4 KiB. `sim/test_p2_icache.py` holds it (budget 500 misses and 1.3 M modelled cycles per tick; two controls fail). Other packs (Chladni) not yet swept.
**P2 status (B-951, host-simulated):** done for the history view: per-column constants (source position, taper window, age band) computed once per geometry into a table instead of per cell per layer per frame, blend calls skipped when off. Commands identical (golden test), instructions per tick 10-38 % fewer (the 2-3x target is not reached: the Catmull-Rom interpolation per layer per cell is 30 % of what is left and cannot be shared without changing the rounding). Costs 1,600 B of the shared on-chip scratch (budget raised to Chladni's 2,816 B).
**P3 status (B-952, host-simulated):** (a) built as a third view, SCROLL (view = 2): scroll-by-copy, newest on the right, flat colour per layer, no taper/age fade, not composed off-screen; 4-19 commands a picture. The look differs from HISTORY, so it is a separate view and nothing existing changed. (b) B21 envelope fill and (c) half-rate redraw not started.
**P1b - restore the time-based cost guard (firmware only; found from the DEV 205 run, B-945).** `layered_wave.inc` has two stride rules: the firmware's B-524 rule (stride widens when one redraw takes more than 9 ms of CPU) under `#ifdef LW_STATS`, and the host's command-count rule (above 300 commands the cell widens by one pixel; the golden test compares this one). The old player defined `LW_STATS` (`fw/player.c:5095`); **Player 2 defines it nowhere, so on the Pocket the meter has been running the command-count rule** (inferred from the code; the shots fit). Consequence, measured on the NEON screenshot of DEV 205 (`20261106_144857.png`, history, 6 layers): **the top edge is 40 flat runs of exactly 8 pixels out of 43**, i.e. a stride of 8, set by the 300-command budget (6 layers x about 50 cells), not by CPU time. With the CPU now about an order of cheaper, the time rule would pick a much finer stride (NEON about 2). Needs: define `LW_STATS` (or move the rule out of the `#ifdef`) in the Player 2 build, keep the host build on the count rule so the golden test stays valid, and re-run the cost-per-stride table; the 12 bytes of statistics sit in the pack's scratch.
**P2 - instruction diet (firmware only).** Precompute per geometry the tables that depend only on width, taper and resolution (the taper window per column, the age band per column, the history position per column) instead of per cell per frame; replace the per-cell divisions by stepping; give the common case (no antialiasing, no blending) its own inner loop without the per-cell `lw_cell`/`lw_bch`/`lw_endlayer` calls. Target 2-3x fewer instructions (0.4-1.1 M down to roughly 0.15-0.4 M cycles). **Constraint:** the JS twin (`tools/meters/preview/meters/layered_wave.js`) is the reference and the golden test compares command for command: either the twin changes in step or the commands stay identical (tables are an optimisation, not a behaviour change).
**P3 - structural, only if P1+P2 are not enough or a visual change is wanted.** (a) Scroll-by-copy history: shift the picture with the one-pass engine scroll and draw only the new columns (about 45 commands at any resolution, spec section 5 "SCROLL"); the cost is that the tail taper and the age colours are position-dependent, so the picture changes (taper clipped, not scaled): **an owner decision on the look**. (b) B21 hardware envelope fill: RTL, a fit, two seeds, a Pocket run; fixes antialiasing as well. (c) Redraw at half rate under load (the governor already exists).
**P4 - limits.** After P1 lower `p2.max_cycles` for Layered Wave from 15 M to a few M, so a regression is struck (the pack watchdog ceiling is 20 M cycles, 300 ms; at 15 M x 4 the window would be the ceiling anyway).
**Order and cost.** P0 first (an hour of owner time, one install). P1 and the test are a day of firmware work in a worktree and cost no VM and no card. P2 follows once P1 is proven. P3 only on the owner's call.

## 6. What this analysis did not establish
1. The model's constants (253 cycles a miss from the simulator figure 31.6 per word; 1.65 cycles an instruction from `HOW_IT_WORKS.md`) are not measured for this code on a Pocket: P0 does that.
2. Whether the built-in Layered Wave of a non-PACKS build has the same conflicts: its object lies in the cold image at other addresses (not modelled; the release ships packs).
3. The draw engine's time for this meter after the CPU fix, and the PSRAM data-window cost of the history ring (6 layers x 101 words x about 32 cycles = about 19 k cycles per redraw [DOC]: small).
4. Other cold code that may evict Layered Wave's lines between ticks (the UI): the model runs the meter alone.
5. The one unreproduced 1.75 M-cycle tick (`TALOS_T4_MEASUREMENT_RESULT_2026-10-27.md`, "Seen outside the pairs") and the DEV 173 7.66 M tick: which preset was running is not recorded.

## 7. Method and reproduction
The scripts used (a profiling copy of `rv32sim.py` with the instruction-cache model, the pack harness for a chosen preset and box, the variant builder, the table generator, and the raw tables) are in `docs/research/lw_perf_2026-11-06/` (they contain absolute paths of the machine they ran on; the README says what to change). Nothing in the firmware, the packs or the RTL was changed by this analysis.

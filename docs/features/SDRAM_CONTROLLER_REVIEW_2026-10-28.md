# SDRAM controller: expert reviews, what changes, and one controller or two (2026-10-28)

> **2026-10-28: superseded where it disagrees with contract v0.2** (`SDRAM_CONTROLLER_CONTRACT.md`): tRFC is 97.5 ns = 10 cycles (not 8), the urgent bound is L_max + 27 (est.), and the order is **Tau V's scheduler first, then the player's** (D-T09; this review's S0..S6 order, player first, is retired).

Inputs: two independent read-only reviews commissioned for this (a memory-controller architect and a video-codec/decoder specialist, both given Tau V's `SDRAM_CONTROLLER_REQUIREMENTS.md` v1 and `docs/features/SDRAM_CONTROLLER_PLAN.md`), their evidence documents, and the repository. The reviewers are model agents; nothing below is accepted on their word alone: each claim that I could check in the repository or by arithmetic is marked **checked**, the rest **reviewer's estimate** (to be confirmed against the datasheet or by a build).

> **Update after reading the datasheet and agg23's sources (`docs/features/SDRAM_PART_FACTS.md`):** the I/O finding stands but is the common practice of working Pocket cores (hygiene and margin, not a fault); the power-up sequence matches the part (the 200 us and 8 refreshes is the deep-power-down exit); **new: the controller's tRFC (80 ns, 9 cycles = 90 ns) is below the datasheet's 97.5 ns**; CL2 is limited to 110 MHz; tAC(CL2) is 8 ns.

## 1. Findings that change the plan (merged, ranked)
| # | Finding | Applies to | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The I/O is not what both documents assume.** `ap_core.qsf` has no `FAST_INPUT_REGISTER`, `FAST_OUTPUT_REGISTER` or `FAST_OUTPUT_ENABLE_REGISTER` on any `dram_*` pin; `p0_q` is the pin combinationally and goes through the arbiter to capture registers in fabric (so Quartus cannot pack them into I/O cells); the SDC has no `set_input_delay` / `set_output_delay` for the SDRAM and the PLL outputs are an asynchronous clock group, so the read-capture and command-launch paths are not timed at all, and the DQM pins are set to 4 mA while the others use the default. `SETTING_USE_FAST_INPUT_REGISTER = 1` only adds a latency cycle. | both documents (my plan said the old controller "has fast input registers") | **checked** (grep of `ap_core.qsf`, both SDC files, `tau_sdram_ctrl.sv:404`) |
| 2 | **The part is a 1.8 V mobile SDR SDRAM, AS4C32M16MSA-6BIN** ("up to 166 MHz" in the Pocket reference; the repo's I/O standard is 1.8 V). Neither document says so. Mobile -6 grades commonly allow CL2 only at a lower clock than the headline (the reviewer's estimate is about 83 to 104 MHz with tAC(CL2) 6 to 8 ns); our controller already runs 100 MHz CL2 and has soaked (A-094, 30 minutes) but may sit at the datasheet edge (skill KB-021 is a low-confidence lead for CL3 above about 85 MHz). A mobile part also has an extended mode register (drive strength, partial-array refresh) that is never programmed today; its power-up sequence (clock-stable time, number of refreshes) must be checked against the datasheet (our controller waits about 101 us and does 2 refreshes). | both | part identity **checked**; the timing limits **to be read from the datasheet** |
| 3 | **Tau V's overhead target contradicts its own acceptance.** 2 cycles per request over 3.74 M requests is +7.5 M cycles, so 33.0 M, but the replay accepts 28.1 M, which leaves 0.70 cycles per request over a model that has no command bus and perfect look-ahead. The plan's "proven by the replay" was wrong. | Tau V section 2 vs 6.3, plan section 2 | **checked** (arithmetic) |
| 4 | **Fixed BL8 is wrong for this traffic (my recommendation 1 is withdrawn).** A sequential BL8 burst wraps inside its aligned 8-word block, so a request that starts at column c touches ceil(((c mod 8) + n) / 8) blocks: on average 1.875 blocks (15 bus cycles) for 8 words, and the 6.4-word median request costs about twice its data unless every burst is cut by the next command (and then "no terminate timing" is false). Also the urgent bound comes from the maximum grant length, not the burst length (the plan's own formula uses 128). | plan section 2 rec 1 | reviewers agree (both) |
| 5 | **SDRAM cannot be back-pressured.** Reads cannot stall; a write bubble either advances the column with DQM high (later words land wrong) or forces a re-issued WRITE. So the write interface is a **pull with a fixed, parameterised lead** (exactly Talos's `wsrc_addr` leads by two cycles) and reads **push without backpressure**; a client that may bubble needs its own FIFO. "Ready/valid for writes" is removed. | Tau V section 3, plan section 2 | memory reviewer |
| 6 | **133/150 MHz is not a parameter change.** The DDIO-inverted chip clock sits half a period from the controller edge; tAC, tCO and board delay are fixed nanoseconds, so above 100 MHz the capture window closes (the reviewer's estimate: about 1.5 ns margin at 100 MHz CL2, about 0.35 ns at 133 MHz CL3, none at 150 MHz CL3). It needs a PLL output with a tuned phase for the chip clock and/or a capture clock, a new VCO for 133 MHz (it is not a divisor of the 600 MHz VCO), CL3, and clock-crossing FIFOs at every port. **CL3 at 100 MHz** is a useful fallback parameter (the reviewer estimates more margin for under 2% throughput with open page). | Tau V section 1, plan rec 4 | reviewer's estimate, to be measured |
| 7 | **The chip model is not an independent check of the I/O.** A zero-delay model clocked by the inverted clock can accept a wrong capture cycle. It needs tAC, tOH, tHZ, tLZ and a programmable board/pin delay, run at the minimum and maximum, and a cross-check against a vendor behavioural model kept outside the repository (test only, never copied: D-P01). Static timing with a real SDC is the only timing proof, because there is no timing-annotated gate-level flow for Cyclone V in Quartus. Remove tWTR (DDR only); keep tWR before PRECHARGE, tCCD 1, and add tRAS(max). | both, section 6 | memory reviewer |
| 8 | **Bus turnaround and DQ contention are missing from both documents:** DQM read latency 2 and write latency 0, read-to-write needs DQM high two cycles before WRITE plus a gap, the chip's edge lags the FPGA's by about 9 ns so the chip may still drive while the FPGA enables, and **a simulation assertion that FPGA output enable and chip drive never overlap must gate every card install.** | both | memory reviewer |
| 9 | **Same-bank collisions:** every buffer base (player's back buffer at word 2^20, Tau V's pictures every 8 MB = 2^22) is in bank 0 with bank = a[11:10], so scan-out(y) vs draw(y) and reference(x, y) vs reconstruction(x, y) always conflict. Make the bank function a parameter with an XOR option (for example a[11:10] ^ a[21:20]) and decide it with a replay that includes scan-out; Tau V can also skew each picture base by k x 2 KB. | both | both reviewers |
| 10 | **The urgent-wait bound is incomplete.** At 100 MHz: W = L_max + about 19 cycles (tWR 2, tRP 2, tRFC 8, tRCD 2, CL 2, capture 1, pipeline 2), 147 cycles at L = 128, far inside both line periods (22.7 us and 41.7 us). The bound breaks if postponed refreshes are repaid as a batch (up to 8 x tRFC): repay one per idle gap, never while urgent; pre-activation is not a grant; urgent port 0 wins every arbitration while held, including the split pieces of its own 400-word line. | both | memory + codec reviewers |
| 11 | **Ordering across ports.** "Completion" must mean every beat has been driven to the chip, otherwise an engine FENCE followed by a CPU read, or a CPU posted write followed by a blit kick, can race. Within a port the scheduler must not reorder. | both | memory reviewer |
| 12 | **Tau V's 60% is a lower bound for average content, and the worst case is not bounded.** The study's numbers recompute (138%, 60%, 88%/38%; the traffic per macroblock 474 words in 74 requests), but: a worst legal macroblock (bi-predicted, quarter-pel) is 752 words in 106 requests, 1.59 times the average; a B picture of them needs about 77% raw (data plus scan-out), **88 to 98% with realistic overhead** (reviewer's estimate); per-request interleaving of the read and write ports maximises read/write turnarounds (+20 to 25%); the four-vector profile planned in Tau V's scope document would need 91% raw, beyond any 100 MHz controller; the 48-picture clip is average content. Honest headroom at 100 MHz: 25 to 35% on average content and about -5 to +10% on the worst picture; the minimum controller efficiency is about 67% (average) and about 89% (worst) at 100 MHz, about 59% at 150 MHz. **The controller is not the only lever and may not be sufficient alone: Tau V needs client-side changes and a level rule** (section 3). | Tau V, section 4 and 6 | codec reviewer (recomputed from the study; the worst-case figures are estimates) |

Smaller: tRCD/tRP/tRAS/tRC/tRFC/tWR are 2/2/5/6/8/2 cycles at 100 MHz (3/3/8/9/12/3 at 150 MHz) when converted in integer picoseconds, and the old controller's `CEIL` macro adds one cycle on exact ratios (tRC 7, tRFC 9, refresh interval 751: harmless, but the new one must use integer arithmetic with `$error` checks); "postpone up to 8 (JEDEC)" is a DDR rule, for SDR it is a debt counter; tRFC for this part may not be 80 ns; look-ahead of 1 is enough only for an in-order model, at CL3 a row miss (9 cycles) is longer than a 4 to 5 word burst so 2 or more is needed; the player's A6 "old-timing mode" is not reproducible as a parameter (keep the old module selectable instead); the model counts refresh twice and misses row conflicts between decode and scan-out (a few percent each, opposite signs).

## 2. One controller or two? (the owner's question)
The two projects are different problems, not two sizes of one:
* **Tau V is throughput-bound:** 58 to 77% of the 200 MB/s peak, requests of 6.4 words, the quality of the scheduler decides whether the decoder runs. It needs open pages, look-ahead, direction batching, possibly 2D requests, possibly a faster clock, and a hard scan-out deadline.
* **The player is latency- and robustness-bound:** scan-out is 17 MB/s of 200 (a 400-word line every 41.7 us), the engine moves long bursts, the CPU needs a few single-word accesses with low latency, and the measurements of DEV 195 to 198 show the controller is not what limits any meter. Its risk is the **one Pocket unit**: any new controller must not corrupt memory the CPU, scanout and engine rely on.

What is the same: the chip, the board, the pins, the clock tree, the power-up and mode-register sequence, the I/O registers and the constraints, the command encodings and legality rules, the chip model and the safety assertions. That is exactly where the memory reviewer's blockers sit (findings 1, 2, 6, 7, 8), and it is where a mistake costs the unit.

**Options**
| Option | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| A. One shared parametric controller (the plan so far) | one set of tests and one soak | every Tau V-driven feature (open page, six ports, batching, 2D requests, a faster clock) becomes a risk to the player's memory path, the player's release is tied to Tau V's schedule, the verification matrix multiplies, and the player gets little of the benefit |
| **B. A shared PHY (pins, I/O registers, clocking, init, command legality, chip model, assertions) with two schedulers on top** | the board-level risk and the constraints are solved and soaked once, on the one unit, by the player's simpler traffic first; each project's scheduler is as simple or as aggressive as its traffic needs; a scheduler bug cannot violate chip timing if the PHY enforces command legality (a safety interlock for the single unit); either project can ship without the other | one more interface to define and keep stable; two schedulers to verify (against the same model and tests); the PHY adds a register stage (a few cycles of latency, which open-page overlap hides for Tau V and the player does not mind) |
| C. Two separate controllers | no coupling at all | the I/O work, the chip model and the safety checks are done twice, the single unit is exposed twice, and the two diverge |

**Recommendation: B.** It also gives the owner the freedom asked for: Tau V's scheduler can be built and changed in Tau V's repository against a frozen PHY and the same chip model, and the player's scheduler stays small. The decision is the owner's; the rest of this document assumes B and marks what changes if A or C is chosen.

## 3. Fit for purpose, per project (the revised requirements)
### Shared PHY and verification (both)
P1 pin registers: one register per output and per-bit output-enable register, input captured in an I/O register (assignments in the QSF, not in RTL alone), CKE high, DQM aligned by the PHY (read latency 2, write latency 0). P2 SDC for the SDRAM: input/output delays from the part's tAC/tOH/tIS/tIH and a board estimate, the chip clock from a PLL output with a programmable phase (the DDIO inverted clock stays the 100 MHz baseline), the DQM drive aligned with the other pins, slack read at slow 85C and fast 0C in every fit. P3 power-up per the datasheet including the extended mode register; re-init (precharge all first) after a reset mid-burst. P4 command legality: the PHY (or a thin guard in front of it) enforces tRCD, tRP, tRAS, tRC, tRRD, tWR, tRFC, tCCD and the read/write turnaround and stalls an illegal command instead of issuing it (and counts it). P5 a chip model with delays (tAC, tOH, tHZ, tLZ, board delay at min and max), timing and legality checks, a data model with byte enables, an OE-overlap assertion, run first against the OLD controller (a baseline finding) and cross-checked against a vendor model kept outside the repository. P6 the part's real CL/tCK table; CL2 and CL3 both supported at 100 MHz; 133/150 MHz only as a later variant with its own PLL phase and clock-crossing at the ports, never "without code changes". P7 refresh as a debt counter (maximum 8), repaid one per idle gap and never while an urgent port waits; tRAS(max) respected for open rows. P8 integer-picosecond timing conversion with elaboration checks.
### The player's scheduler (Tau Alpha)
Three ports (scanout fill urgent, the draw engine, the CPU bridge), requests 1 to 512 words with page-crossing split, byte enables, the mailbox and `sdram_init_complete` semantics unchanged, the engine's pull-address write interface (its `wsrc_addr` lead of two) kept, simple open-page with the bank function chosen by the player's own replay (including scan-out), per-port counters on MMIO (replacing the busy counter), completion = every beat driven, no reordering within a port, urgent bound stated in cycles (about 147 at 128 words). Acceptance: the player's own recorded traffic replay (not slower than the old controller, CPU word latency lower), `GFX SCAN LATE` 0 on a Pocket, A-094 before and after, the 30-minute soak, the owner's warm soak.
### Tau V's scheduler (Tau V, on the shared PHY)
Open page, look-ahead 2 or more, **direction batching** (write-drain watermark or one ordered decoder port) instead of per-request round robin, optional 2D requests, maximum grants of at least the write-buffer line, published bank function with a picture-base skew rule, command-bus occupancy counted in the model, **acceptance rewritten**: a real-time replay at Level 3 with per-picture deadlines, the stated decode-ahead and all ports at once (scan-out, CPU, bitstream/audio DMA, refresh), pass = no missed deadline and no underflow with at least 10% slack at 100 MHz; plus worst-case traces (all-bi quarter-pel B pictures with the worst alignment, a static all-skip scene, a pan, a scene change, an I-picture burst, four-vector bi-prediction if the profile keeps it), per-picture cycle distribution (p50/p99/max) over the whole film, and 640/720-wide content.
### Client-side changes Tau V should make first (the codec reviewer's estimates; Tau V's call)
A level rule (cap reference words per picture or forbid bi-prediction with four vectors: bounds the worst picture near 550 words per macroblock, about 63% raw); decode-ahead of 1 to 2 pictures; interleaved Cb/Cr planes (-23% requests, no area); a 4-macroblock write buffer (about 3 M10K: 32-word writes, 6 requests per macroblock instead of 24 to 32, and no turnaround problem); 2D motion-compensation requests; a small reference cache (about 2 M10K for horizontal reuse; about 8 M10K total with row buffers); no whole-picture post pass in SDRAM (+26% at Level 3); scan-out at 48 Hz for 24 fps content. With the first five, the controller can afford 3 to 4 cycles of overhead per request, which makes the original target and acceptance consistent again.

## 5. Corrections to the Tau Alpha plan (`SDRAM_CONTROLLER_PLAN.md`)
Withdrawn or changed: "the old controller has fast input registers" (it does not); recommendation 1 (fixed BL8: wrong, see finding 4); "ready/valid write handshake" (finding 5); "the +2 overhead is proven by the replay" (finding 3); "133/150 as a parameter" (finding 6); the claim that a closed-page A6 mode reproduces the old timing; the single-controller premise (section 2 above); the omission of the part identity, turnaround, ordering semantics and the bank-collision issue. Kept: the player is not bandwidth-bound; the CPU port first keeps single-word semantics (with the 32-bit access as one 2-word request with four byte enables, and writes acknowledged on completion); the bank function internal to the controller; the clean-room process; the old controller selectable until the soak passes.

## 7. Revised reply for Tau V (to pass on, not a shared file)
"Thank you for the requirements; we had them reviewed by a memory-controller architect and a codec specialist. Main points: (1) the part is a 1.8 V mobile SDR SDRAM (AS4C32M16MSA-6BIN) and our current I/O is neither registered in I/O cells nor constrained, so we will first fix and measure that on our side; (2) we propose a **shared PHY (pins, clocking, init, legality, chip model with delays) plus a separate scheduler per project**, so each can be as simple or as aggressive as its traffic needs; you would build the scheduler on a frozen PHY tag; (3) your section 2 overhead target (2 cycles per request) and your section 6.3 acceptance (28.1 M cycles) are inconsistent (the acceptance allows 0.7 cycles per request over the model); we suggest a real-time Level 3 replay with per-picture deadlines instead; (4) please add worst-case traces (all-bi quarter-pel B pictures, static scenes, scene changes) and decide the four-vector profile before freezing the requirement: it needs about 91% of the raw bus; (5) client-side changes that cut the load most cheaply: a level cap on reference words, decode-ahead, interleaved Cb/Cr, a 4-macroblock write buffer, 2D requests; (6) fixed BL8 is wrong for 6-word requests, we will use full-page with terminate or chained BL8 with interrupts; writes are a pull with a fixed lead and reads push without back-pressure; (7) 133/150 MHz needs a PLL phase variant and CL3, so treat it as a later experiment on your own test core. Timeline: PHY contract and chip model first, then the PHY and the player's scheduler, then a tag you can build on."
